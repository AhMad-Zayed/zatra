# Storefront Product Vision — What Zatara Needs to Actually Compete

**Author's stance:** this document is written as if I were the owner of Zatara Tourism, not the engineer who built it. Investigation only — nothing below was implemented as part of this pass. It's grounded in three things: (1) a live walkthrough of the real running storefront against the real dev database on 2026-08-31 (homepage, catalog search, trip details, full checkout, login, footer/legal), (2) a full read of this repo's own prior audits (`STOREFRONT_UX_AUDIT.md`, `UX_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md`) and the decisions already on record (`PRD.md`, `FUTURE_FEATURES_BACKLOG.md`), and (3) standard 2025–2026 travel-e-commerce conventions — the patterns Booking.com/Airbnb/Kayak-class sites share regardless of destination market.

**What I will not re-litigate:** reviews are deferred until real bookings exist, no fake ratings ever. "Most Booked" is approved (auto-computed) but unbuilt. "Exclusive Offer" is approved (manual toggle) but unbuilt. Last-minute deals are deferred pending a real pricing-rules engine. No invented content — real photos and real copy only. Everything below is built on top of those decisions, not around them.

---

## The one fact that should shape every decision in this document

**The entire live catalog is one trip.** Maldives Luxury Package, $5,000/person, one departure date, one photo placeholder, English title and description on an otherwise Arabic site. A second template ("العقبة") exists in the database but was never linked into the catalog. Every UX judgment below — filters, badges, "browse destinations," comparison, IA — has to be read through that lens. A storefront audit normally asks "does this convert browsers into bookers." Right now the honest answer is: **there is nothing to browse.** No amount of UI polish fixes a catalog of one. I'll say this again in Section D because it's the single most important prioritization fact in this whole report.

---

## A. Information architecture & layout

**The skeleton is right.** Homepage → catalog → trip details → checkout → confirmation → my-bookings is the same shape every real OTA uses, and the checkout wizard's four steps (contact info → passengers → add-ons/rooms → payment) match the standard modern pattern almost exactly: capture the low-friction stuff first (name/email/phone), delay the highest-friction stuff (passenger documents) to step 2 with a visible seat-hold countdown already running so the customer feels urgency without being blocked, then optional upsells, then payment last. That ordering is genuinely well thought out and I wouldn't change it.

**Trip details page structure is also correct** — price-forward header, hero image, key facts (dates, duration), description, itinerary, a pricing table, sticky booking widget. This is the right shape for a considered high-ticket purchase (this is a $5,000 product, not a $20 impulse buy — customers need to see price, dates, and duration within the first screen, and they do).

Where the IA is wrong for what actually exists today:

- **The homepage hero search (destination text field + date picker) and the catalog's filter bar (داخلي/خارجي toggle, price-range control) are built for a catalog that doesn't exist.** I live-tested all three: destination search works now (this was the critical crash bug from the prior audit — confirmed fixed, no more HTTP 500), but typing "Maldives" and searching just re-shows the one card that was already there. The داخلي/خارجي (domestic/international) toggle does nothing observable when clicked — no visual selected state, no filtering — which tracks with the architecture audit's finding that `trip_type` doesn't exist anywhere in the data model. **A filter that visibly does nothing when clicked is worse for trust than no filter at all** — it reads as broken, not as "coming soon." With one trip, none of this filtering apparatus can prove itself either way, but a first-time visitor doesn't know that; they just experience a search bar and two filter tabs that don't do anything.
- **No "what's included / what's not" section on trip details.** Every OTA I know of separates this from the raw price table — flights, hotel, meals, transport, insurance, in vs. out. Right now a customer sees a price and a passenger-category table but has to guess or WhatsApp the agency to find out what's actually covered on a $5,000 package. This is a real, standalone IA gap, independent of the missing-content problem — it needs a place to live even before real copy is written for it.
- **"وجهاتنا" (Our Destinations) and "عن زتارة" (About Zatara)** are both live nav items — desktop and mobile — pointing at `href="#"`. They're present in the IA (so customers expect them) but don't exist as pages. A customer clicking "About Zatara" before a $5,000 purchase and landing nowhere is a real trust ding, not cosmetic.
- **The checkout wizard has a running total on steps 2–3 but no persistent order-summary sidebar** the way step 4 gets a dedicated dark summary card. Minor, but on a 4-step, multi-field flow for a high-ticket item, customers benefit from being able to see "what am I actually paying" at every step, not just the last one.

## B. Visual design direction

The honest answer: **the design system itself is good — better than a generic template — but the live execution right now does not read as premium, because of a mix of real bugs and near-total content emptiness.** Specifics, not vibes:

1. **The primary WhatsApp contact button is invisible on the homepage.** `storefront.blade.php:106` combines a `glass-panel` class (which, per the Phase A "de-glassing" comment already in the code, resolves to a *solid white* background, not a translucent one) with `text-white`. On the homepage's unscrolled hero state, that's white text and a white icon on a white button — I confirmed this live and by zooming into the rendered pixels: the button is a blank white pill with no visible content. This is the PRD's explicitly-named primary support channel ("WhatsApp-first communication"), broken on the highest-traffic page in the site, on the very first screen a visitor sees.
2. **That same WhatsApp button — and the "اتصل بنا" nav link, in all four places it appears (desktop nav, desktop CTA button, mobile drawer nav, mobile drawer CTA) — links to a hardcoded fallback number (`970599000000`), not the tenant's real number.** I traced this to the code: the header reads `$currentTenant->phone`, but there is no `phone` column on the `tenants` table at all (confirmed against every tenant migration) — it silently resolves to `null` and falls through to the hardcoded fallback, every time, for every tenant. Meanwhile, a real, already-built admin page (`ManageAgencySettings`) *does* let the business set a real contact number, under the key `settings['whatsapp_number']` — but nothing in the header reads that key. So even after the business fills in their real WhatsApp number (which the footer's social-icons block *does* read correctly), the two most prominent WhatsApp touchpoints on every single page keep showing a fake placeholder number. This is a genuine, code-level inconsistency, not a content gap — two different, disconnected sources of truth for the same piece of information on the same page.
3. **Dates render visually scrambled on the trip details page.** The DOM text is correct — `04 Sep, 2026 - 09 Sep, 2026` (confirmed via extracted page text) — but on screen, under the trip title, it renders as `Sep, 2026 - 09 Sep, 2026 04`. This is a classic bidi (bidirectional text) bug: a Latin/numeric date string sitting inside an RTL-directional page without `dir="ltr"` isolation gets visually reordered by the browser. It's the second thing a shopper reads on the page (right under the trip name) and it looks glitchy even though nothing is functionally wrong. I did not see this same bug on the checkout summary card's date, which suggests it's isolated to `trip-details.blade.php:45` specifically, not systemic.
4. **The footer's "تواصل معنا" (Contact Us) column is genuinely empty** — no phone, no address, no hours, no social icons — on every page. I confirmed this is *not* a code gap: the Blade template already conditionally renders phone/address/hours/Facebook/Instagram/TikTok/WhatsApp from `$currentTenant->settings`, and there's already a working admin settings page to fill those values in. It's empty purely because nobody has filled the form in yet. Worth stating plainly because it's the cheapest fix in this entire document — literally filling in one admin form — and it currently means the last thing a visitor sees on every page before leaving the site is a blank column.
5. **The hero copy promises something the product can't currently back up.** "اكتشف أروع الوجهات حول العالم بتجربة حجز فائقة السلاصة والرفاهية" ("discover the world's finest destinations with a supremely seamless, luxurious booking experience") sits above a catalog of one trip whose image is a gradient-and-icon placeholder labeled "الصورة قريباً" (photo coming soon). A $5,000 "luxury" product with no real photo is a much bigger credibility problem than any layout issue — it's the single biggest thing undermining the "premium" positioning the copy is going for.
6. **English content on an Arabic-first site.** "Maldives Luxury Package" and "A luxurious 5-night stay in the Maldives." are the only non-Arabic strings on an otherwise fully right-to-left, fully Arabic page. It reads as an unfinished translation even though it's a content decision, not a bug.
7. **Where the design system actually has something to show, it's solid.** The navy/gold/orange palette, card shadows, the dark "ملخص الحجز" order-summary card in checkout, the step-progress indicator, the room-price display (now correctly showing per-room shared/single pricing — confirmed live, this was a real bug in the prior audit and is now fixed) — these all look like a considered, coherent brand, not a template. The problem is proportion: right now the polish is being spent on a shell with almost nothing real inside it.

**Verdict:** not generic-template-bad, but not premium-trustworthy yet either — it's a good design system currently exposing real bugs and an almost-empty catalog. Fixing the three concrete bugs above and populating the one settings form would measurably change how the site reads, before a single new feature gets built.

## C. Missing features/sections — prioritized, with honest effort/value calls

For each: **(i)** real engineering gap worth building now, **(ii)** blocked on real business data/content the stakeholder must supply, **(iii)** a bigger product decision that needs to be scoped on its own before any code gets written.

1. **The three bugs in Section B** (invisible WhatsApp button, phone/whatsapp-number source mismatch, bidi date scramble) — **(i)**. Hours, not days. No content dependency. High value because it directly protects the PRD's named primary support channel.
2. **Fill in the Manage Agency Settings form** (contact phone, WhatsApp number, address, hours, social links) — **(ii)**. Zero engineering; the form already exists. This is the single highest-leverage, lowest-effort item in this entire document, and it's not mine to do — it's a five-minute task for whoever owns the business's real contact details.
3. **Real photos + real Arabic copy (description, itinerary, FAQ) for the one live trip** — **(ii)**. This is the actual blocker on the site looking "premium" rather than "unfinished." No engineering effort beyond upload; entirely gated on the stakeholder producing or sourcing real assets.
4. **More real, bookable trips in the catalog** — **(ii)**, arguably the most important item in this whole document and *not a UX problem at all*. A single-item catalog caps everything: search, filters, comparison, badges, "most booked" — none of it can mean anything until there's more than one real thing to sell. This is an inventory/ops decision for the business, not a build item for engineering.
5. **"What's included / not included" section on trip details** — **(i)**, small-to-medium effort, standard OTA pattern. Real, standalone gap independent of the content problem above; reduces WhatsApp back-and-forth about what's covered on a high-ticket item.
6. **Real "About," "Destinations," and "Contact" pages** behind the three currently-dead nav links — mostly **(ii)** (company story, real photos, real contact details) with a thin **(i)** page-shell wrapper once that content exists. Don't build the shell first and leave it empty; that's the same "reads unfinished" problem as the trip card.
7. **Multi-photo gallery per trip** (not just one hero image) — mixed **(i)**/**(ii)**: needs a gallery UI (small effort) plus real photos (blocked on the business). Every serious OTA shows 5–10+ images minimum; one hero image is thin even once it's a real photo.
8. **Domestic/international filter tabs and price-range filter** — currently decorative, confirmed non-functional live, and the underlying `trip_type` concept doesn't exist in the schema at all (architecture audit, Section 1.0). This is **(iii)** — a real schema/product decision (should trip type be a first-class validated field, tied to document requirements?) — not a quick wire-up. **My recommendation: remove the two tabs for now rather than leave them decorative.** A visibly non-functional control does more trust damage than a missing one, and with one trip there's nothing to filter anyway.
9. **Child/infant fare tiers** — turns out to be **(ii)**, not a schema gap. I checked: `trip_passenger_categories` is already a free-text name + price table with no fixed category enum — an admin can add a "طفل (Child)" row at a lower price through the existing UI today. The only thing missing is a business decision on child pricing policy and someone entering it. Cheap to unblock once that policy exists.
10. **"Most Booked" / "Exclusive Offer" badges** — already approved per project decisions, and technically ready to build **(i)**, but I'd sequence this *after* catalog depth (item 4), not before. "Most Booked," computed from real booking counts, is meaningless with one trip to compare against, and "Exclusive Offer" on the only item in the store isn't exclusive, it's just the store. Building the logic now would decorate an empty shelf.
11. **Online payment gateway** — **(iii)**, a real infrastructure and business decision (which PSP — mada/Visa/Mastercard/Apple Pay for this market, fees, PCI/compliance posture, timeline), already flagged in the prior audit and explicitly stubbed out in code (`abort(403, 'Stripe payments are currently disabled.')`). I'm flagging it again here because it's the single highest-value *unlock* for actual conversion: today, 100% of online bookings require the customer to leave the digital flow and complete a cash or bank-transfer step before their seat is secured. No UX change fixes that; only a real gateway integration does.
12. **Real rooming/multi-hotel support** (per-passenger room assignment, room-type inventory, multiple hotels per trip) — **(iii)**, a major redesign per the architecture audit; the current flat `PackageOption` tier already covers a single-hotel trip correctly (and now shows real per-room pricing, confirmed live). Don't touch this until the business is actually running multi-hotel trips often enough to need it.
13. **Persistent order-summary sidebar across all checkout steps** — **(i)**, small polish item, moderate value. Not urgent.

## D. If I owned this business: the roadmap, in order

Engineering time is scarce, same as any real startup — this is deliberately short, not exhaustive.

**Phase 0 — this week, code-only, hours not days.**
Fix the three concrete bugs from Section B: make the WhatsApp CTA button actually visible on the homepage hero; point every header/nav WhatsApp/contact link at `settings['whatsapp_number']` instead of the nonexistent `phone` column; fix the bidi date rendering on trip details. Zero content dependency, protects the PRD's named primary channel, and it's the cheapest set of fixes in this whole document relative to their visibility.

**Phase 1 — in parallel, but this is a business task, not an engineering one.**
Fill in the Manage Agency Settings form (real phone, WhatsApp number, address, hours, social links — the admin page already exists and is empty). Get real photos and real Arabic copy (description, a real itinerary, 2–3 real FAQs) for the one live trip. This is the highest-leverage item in the entire document and it costs zero engineering hours. Until this happens, no amount of further UX or feature work will make the site read as "premium" — the gap is content, not code.

**Phase 2 — small, parallelizable engineering, once Phase 1 content starts landing.**
Build the "what's included/not included" section on trip details. Remove the decorative domestic/international and price-range filter tabs (or gate them behind a feature flag until there's a real catalog to filter). Add a child fare-tier pricing row once the business sets a child pricing policy — this is mostly data entry, not a build.

**Phase 3 — the real conversion unlock, business decision first, then engineering.**
Scope and integrate a real online payment gateway for this market. This is the one change most likely to move actual completed-booking numbers, because right now every interested online customer hits a mandatory offline step before their seat is secured. Start with the business decision (which PSP, what it costs, what compliance it requires) — don't let engineering guess at this.

**Phase 4 — gated on catalog depth, not on a date.**
Once there are genuinely 3–5+ real, bookable trips (an inventory/ops milestone the business controls, not an engineering sprint): build the already-approved "Most Booked" (auto-computed) and "Exclusive Offer" (manual toggle) badges, and revisit whether the domestic/international filter is worth wiring up for real. Doing any of this against today's one-trip catalog produces no visible value no matter how well it's built.

**Phase 5 — ongoing, gated on real bookings.**
Reviews/ratings, exactly as already decided: only once there's real customer feedback to show, never fabricated.

## E. What I would explicitly not build right now

- **Reviews/ratings before real bookings exist.** Already the right call on record — restating it here because it's the correct call, not a gap.
- **Last-minute deals before a real pricing-rules/discount engine is designed.** Also already deferred correctly. A countdown-timer "flash sale" bolted onto the store's one $5,000 trip would look desperate, not exciting, on top of the schema risk.
- **"Most Booked"/"Exclusive Offer" badge logic before catalog depth exists.** Approved, but building it today decorates an empty shelf — sequence it behind Phase 1/4 above, not before.
- **A real multi-hotel/per-passenger rooming system.** No evidence the business runs multi-hotel trips often enough to justify the schema work the architecture audit says this needs; today's single-hotel package tier is fine and is already showing correct pricing.
- **Bus/fleet assignment.** Same reasoning — no data model exists for it today, and there's no evidence the business needs one; `available_seats` already covers a single trip's capacity correctly.
- **Finishing Google/social login.** It's stubbed "قريباً" and the underlying package (`laravel/socialite`) isn't even installed. Phone-based OTP login already works and matches the PRD's phone-first architecture; this is not worth engineering time while there's a single-trip catalog and no payment gateway to prioritize instead.
- **Formalizing domestic/international as a validated system field.** Worth doing eventually if the business ends up running genuinely different document/requirement rules per trip type, but that's a real schema decision (per the architecture audit) in search of a second real trip type to justify it. Don't build the schema speculatively.
- **Any further visual "richness" pass** (more hero animation, more glassmorphism, more hover effects). The existing design system is already good enough that more polish on top of one trip with no real photo doesn't move the needle. The bottleneck right now is content and the three concrete bugs above, not design ambition.
- **Wishlist / saved trips / trip comparison tools.** These matter once shoppers are genuinely comparing across a real catalog. They're premature on a catalog of one, and standard OTA UX doesn't need them until catalog depth exists.
